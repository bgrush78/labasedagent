## Where Rates Actually Are

The average 30-year fixed was 7.4% for the week through Wednesday, October 14, per Freddie Mac, the highest level since late 2023. A year ago, that same loan averaged 6.27%. And back in February? The 52-week low was 5.90%, on February 27, 2026. So yes, it's been a rough few months.

Here's what that looks like in real money. Take a $900,000 home with 20% down, a $720,000 loan:

| 30-Year Rate | Monthly Principal & Interest |
|---|---|
| 5.90% (Feb 2026 low) | $4,271 |
| 6.27% (a year ago) | $4,443 |
| 7.40% (today) | $4,985 |

That's about $540 more a month than last fall. Roughly one nice dinner per week.

## What This Means in LA

Here's the part the doom headlines skip: higher rates have thinned out the competition. LA County home prices have stayed essentially flat to slightly up through August and September, with Redfin putting the median near $925,000, up 1.8% year over year. Prices aren't crashing, but sellers aren't calling all the shots anymore either.

About 37% of LA County condo listings carried a price reduction in early October, a record level in some datasets. Translation: there are motivated sellers out there, and they'd like to talk to you. Meanwhile, inventory is still tight, with C.A.R.'s Unsold Inventory Index at 3.9 months in August, down from 4.3 a year earlier, so the good stuff still moves.

## What This Means in the San Gabriel Valley

The SGV is cooling off just enough to be interesting. In the city of San Gabriel, prices over the three months ending August were down 1.5% year over year at a $1.1M median, and homes are taking about 42 days to sell versus 26 a year ago. More days on market means more room to negotiate: inspections, credits, the works.

And there's still real value in the valley. Mid-market cities like Rosemead, Monterey Park, San Gabriel, and Monrovia have single-family medians in the $850,000–$950,000 range, while El Monte sits around $720,000. Bonus: the dumplings are better than anything you'll find in a higher-rate environment anywhere else.

## How Smart Buyers Are Playing It

**Negotiate the rate, not just the price.** Ask the seller for credits toward a rate buydown. On that same $720K loan, a 2-1 buydown starting at 7.40% drops your payment to about $4,043 in year one and $4,504 in year two. A $20K price cut saves you way less per month than putting that money toward the rate.

**Marry the house, date the rate.** You can refinance later if rates fall. You can't go back and buy the house you passed on once the bidding wars return.

**Shop where sellers are motivated.** Listings sitting 45+ days, condos with price drops, homes that need a little love. That's where the leverage is.

**Make the property pay you back.** An ADU, a duplex, or a house with a rentable unit can offset a big chunk of that higher payment. This is my favorite trick, and LA's ADU rules make it more doable than most people think.

**Get a lender who'll actually explain your options.** FHA, VA, ARMs, buydowns. The "right" loan depends on how long you'll stay and what you can put down. Run the numbers before you rule yourself out.
